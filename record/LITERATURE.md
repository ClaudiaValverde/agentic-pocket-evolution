# LITERATURE.md — prior work and datasets for pocket evolution across AFDB

Focused review for the project in `PROMPT.md`. Compiled 2026-10-30.

Verification policy: every reference was checked against Crossref and/or Europe PMC metadata (authors, year, venue, DOI), or against the live primary web page. Numbers are quoted from abstracts or retrieved full texts, not from memory. Unverifiable items were dropped. Preprints are marked *(preprint)*. Dataset versions/sizes/licences were read from live pages on 2026-10-30.

## 1. Evolution of ligand-binding sites and pockets

### Enzyme superfamilies: conserved chemistry, shifting specificity
- **Babbitt & Gerlt 1997**, J Biol Chem, doi:10.1074/jbc.272.49.30591. In mechanistically diverse superfamilies, what is conserved is a partial reaction (chemistry) and the residues that perform it, while substrate specificity varies among families. This is the canonical model for "pocket conserved in position, changed in specificity" (our Part B category 3).
- **Akiva et al. 2014** (SFLD), Nucleic Acids Res, doi:10.1093/nar/gkt1130. Curates superfamily / subgroup / family hierarchies together with conserved active-site residues; provides reference sets of specificity shifts on a conserved scaffold.
- **Furnham, Dawson, Rahman, Thornton & Orengo 2016**, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2015.11.010. Compiled all experimentally annotated function changes in 379 CATH superfamilies. Some superfamilies change reaction without changing catalytic machinery; in others large functional change came with large machinery change; and in some, relatives do similar things with *different* machinery.
- **Dessailly, Dawson, Mizuguchi & Orengo 2013**, BBA Proteins Proteomics, doi:10.1016/j.bbapap.2013.02.042. Quantitative analysis of where functional sites sit in domain superfamilies: catalytic sites occupy very few topological locations, small-ligand sites are "more diverse than expected", protein–protein interfaces most plastic. Implication: across homologs, expect pocket turnover mostly at non-catalytic sites.
- **Panjkovich & Daura 2010**, BMC Struct Biol, doi:10.1186/1472-6807-10-9. Pocket-conservation measure across >1,000 families; concluded "structurally conserved pockets are a common feature of protein families". An early per-family analogue of matching pockets across homologs.
- **Najmanovich 2017**, Curr Opin Struct Biol, doi:10.1016/j.sbi.2016.11.024. Short review of divergent vs convergent shaping of binding sites and of competition between similar ligands/sites.

### Promiscuity, evolvability, ancestral reconstruction
- **Aharoni et al. (Tawfik) 2005**, Nat Genet, doi:10.1038/ng1482. Promiscuous side activities are highly evolvable: few mutations raise them greatly with little cost to the native activity. Pocket "function change" can precede sequence divergence.
- **Tokuriki & Tawfik 2009**, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1169375. Conformational dynamism underlies evolvability of new functions — relevant to cryptic/transient pockets.
- **Khersonsky & Tawfik 2010**, Annu Rev Biochem, doi:10.1146/annurev-biochem-030409-143718. Review of mechanistic and evolutionary aspects of promiscuity.
- **Copley 2017**, Curr Opin Struct Biol, doi:10.1016/j.sbi.2017.11.001. Review of enzyme promiscuity ("Shining a light on enzyme promiscuity").
- **Thornton, Need & Crews 2003**, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1086185. Ancestral steroid receptor resurrection: the ancestral receptor was estrogen-sensitive; specificity for other steroids arose later. The template for "ancestral state vs derived state" tests.
- **Bridgham, Carroll & Thornton 2006**, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1123348. Molecular exploitation: the mineralocorticoid receptor's ligand specificity predates its hormone.
- **Ortlund et al. 2007**, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1142819. Crystal structure of a resurrected ancestral receptor; specificity shift required permissive substitutions (conformational epistasis). Shows that a pocket change can be invisible in the pocket itself and encoded by distant residues.
- **Eick et al. (Thornton) 2012**, PLoS Genet, doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1003072. Evolution of minimal specificity and promiscuity in steroid receptors.
- **Harms & Thornton 2013**, Nat Rev Genet, doi:10.1038/nrg3540. Programmatic review of evolutionary biochemistry: combine ancestral reconstruction with biophysical tests. Methodological backbone for our "ancestral vs derived + co-folding" design.

### Cryptic pockets and their conservation
- **Cimermancic et al. (Sali) 2016** (CryptoSite), J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2016.01.029. Characterisation and prediction of cryptic binding sites — sites invisible in apo structures.
- **Vajda et al. 2018**, Curr Opin Chem Biol, doi:10.1016/j.cbpa.2018.05.003. Definition, detection and druggability of cryptic sites.
- **Meller et al. (Bowman) 2023** (PocketMiner), Nat Commun, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-36699-3. Graph neural network predicting cryptic-pocket locations from a *single* structure — directly applicable to AFDB models, where only one conformer exists.
- Cryptic-pocket opening can vary between homologs and track function: the β-lactamase Ω-loop pocket is not conserved across the family, and homologs that open it have higher penicillinase activity (*(preprint)* bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2021.04.14.439842); for Ebola VP35, homologs open the cryptic pocket with different probabilities and opening is tied to dsRNA binding (eLife reviewed preprint, https://elifesciences.org/articles/104514v1).

### AFDB-scale pocket / ligandability studies
- **Jumper et al. 2021**, Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2; **Varadi et al. 2024** (AFDB in 2024), Nucleic Acids Res, doi:10.1093/nar/gkad1011 (>214M models).
- **Akdel et al. 2022**, Nat Struct Mol Biol, doi:10.1038/s41594-022-00849-w. Community assessment of AF2. Key pocket result (full text): on 225 proteins with apo/holo references, AF2 pockets with mean pocket pLDDT > 90 overlap known sites as well as pockets from experimental structures, whereas low-confidence pockets (mean pLDDT ≤ 90) generally do not overlap known sites and are larger. **This is our pLDDT threshold precedent.**
- **Hekkelman et al. (Perrakis) 2023** (AlphaFill), Nat Methods, doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01685-y. Transplants ligands/cofactors from experimental homologs into AF models: 12,029,789 transplants on 995,411 models at alphafill.eu. Gives an independent, homology-based "is there a ligand here" signal for our pockets.
- **Comajuncosa-Creus et al. (Aloy) 2024** (PocketVec), Nat Commun, doi:10.1038/s41467-024-52146-3. Inverse-virtual-screening pocket descriptors; >32,000 druggable sites over 20,000 human protein domains from experimental + AF2 structures, with >1.2 billion pairwise comparisons, revealing pocket similarities missed by sequence or global-structure methods.
- **Zillmer & Walther 2025**, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013298. Pocketomes of eleven species from AFDB (223,091 structures), pockets predicted and embedded in a 128-D feature space. Headline result: a **sub-linear scaling law** — the number of unique binding sites grows more slowly than the number of unique structures per species. Closest published work to our Part A, but species-level, not ortholog-resolved.
- **Lau et al. 2024** (The Encyclopedia of Domains, TED), Science, doi:10.1126/science.adq4946 — domain parsing of all AFDB; and **Barrio-Hernandez et al. 2023**, Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06510-w — Foldseek clustering of AFDB into 2.30M non-singleton clusters, 31% unannotated, ~4% species-specific clusters that "represent lower-quality predictions or examples of de novo gene birth". **Directly relevant: species-specific clusters are a known artifact/novelty confound for apparent pocket gains.**
- **Durairaj et al. 2023**, Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06622-3. New families and folds in AFDB "dark matter"; useful for deciding which lineage-specific pockets are real novelty.
- **van Kempen et al. 2024** (Foldseek), Nat Biotechnol, doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01773-0. The scalable structure search underlying all of the above.
- **Castillo & Ollila 2025** (ActSeek), Bioinformatics, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf424. Active-site-environment search over AFDB; reports that Foldseek returns hundreds of globally similar proteins of which only a few carry the relevant site — i.e. global structure similarity is a poor proxy for site identity. (Non-commercial licence.)
- *(preprint/submitted)* **AHoJ-AlphaFold**, "A tale of two states: AlphaFold binding pockets on the apo–holo continuum", code https://github.com/ioChris/ahoj-alphafold, data doi:10.5281/zenodo.23243349, built on **AHoJ-DB** (Feidakis et al. 2024, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2024.168545; 468,293 protein–ligand interactions over 27,983 proteins). Places AFDB pockets on an apo↔holo continuum and shows pocket-detection success depends on that class. Treat the preprint claims as unreviewed, but AHoJ-DB itself is peer-reviewed and usable.

**Gap / what is new for us.** Everything above is either per-family (Panjkovich, Dessailly), per-species (Zillmer & Walther), or per-proteome (PocketVec, human only). Nobody has mapped individual pockets onto gene and species trees across orthologs/paralogs and inferred pocket presence/absence at ancestral nodes with an explicit gain/loss model. The pLDDT-stratified pocket validation (Akdel) and the apo/holo-state caveat (AHoJ) exist but have not been used as *controls for inferred evolutionary events*.

## 2. Convergent evolution of binding sites

- **Wallace, Laskowski & Thornton 1996**, Protein Sci, doi:10.1002/pro.5560050603. 3D coordinate templates for the Ser-His-Asp triad; found the triad geometry in unrelated folds. The founding quantitative treatment of the chymotrypsin/subtilisin convergence.
- **Russell 1998**, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1006/jmbi.1998.1844. Systematic detection of 3D side-chain patterns; reported new examples of convergent evolution beyond the triad.
- **Galperin, Walker & Koonin 1998**, Genome Res, doi:10.1101/gr.8.8.779. Analogous enzymes: 105 EC numbers with enzymes lacking detectable sequence similarity (243 proteins); in 34 cases different folds confirmed independent origin.
- **Omelchenko, Galperin, Wolf & Koonin 2010**, Biol Direct, doi:10.1186/1745-6150-5-31. Updated non-homologous isofunctional enzymes (NISE): **185 EC nodes** with two or more structurally unrelated proteins; TIM barrel and Rossmann folds over-represented.
- **Gherardini, Wass, Helmer-Citterich & Sternberg 2007**, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2007.06.017. Catalytic Site Atlas × SCOP. Mechanistic analogues found in **15% (26/169)** of 3-digit EC groups; transformational analogues for **45 of 951** 4-digit EC numbers, about half of which also show active-site convergence; triad-like machinery present in **23 superfamilies**. The key prior frequency estimate for active-site convergence.
- **Riziotis, Kafas, Ong, Borkakoti, Ribeiro & Thornton 2025**, FEBS J, doi:10.1111/febs.17332. Modern curated catalogue: **34 cases** of convergence with sufficient evidence, from an M-CSA-derived set of 6,345 EC-matched pairs reduced to **2,209 unique analogue pairs**; only **9–15% of pairs are homologues** (same CATH superfamily), the rest analogues. They stress that sequence, structure, active-site geometry *and* mechanism must all be checked before calling convergence. This is our gold-standard positive set and our evidential checklist.
- **Hackett, Riziotis, Larralde, Ribeiro, Zeller & Thornton 2026**, Protein Sci, doi:10.1002/pro.70744. A library of **6,780 3D catalytic-site templates** from 762 M-CSA mechanisms, with geometric matching that does not assume sequence or ancestry — usable directly on predicted structures to flag convergent-looking sites.
- Cofactor-site convergence, quantitative: **Moodie, Mitchell & Thornton 1996**, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1006/jmbi.1996.0591 (adenylate recognition as a "fuzzy template" across unrelated proteins); **Denessiouk et al. 2001**, Proteins, doi:10.1002/prot.1093 (shared adenine-recognition motif across ATP/CoA/NAD/NADP/FAD proteins); **Dym & Eisenberg 2001**, Protein Sci, doi:10.1110/ps.12801 (four distinct FAD-binding folds); **Smith, Kahraman & Thornton 2010**, Proteins, doi:10.1002/prot.22747 (heme sites in non-homologous proteins differ strongly in shape, volume and chemistry by function).
- Counterweight — physics, not history: **Gao & Skolnick 2013**, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003302, and **Skolnick & Gao 2013**, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1300011110. ~20,000 non-redundant pockets are covered by **about 1,000 representative pocket shapes** (~400 in the PNAS artificial-structure comparison), essentially every native pocket has a significant match in a library of pockets from *computationally generated, never-selected* structures, and similar pockets recur across fold classes. Their explicit conclusion: the co-occurrence of global-structure, pocket and sequence similarity "significantly underestimates the random background probability" of an evolutionary relationship. **For us: pocket similarity between unrelated folds is the null, not the discovery; convergence claims need a calibrated background.**
- **Doolittle 1994**, Trends Biochem Sci, doi:10.1016/0968-0004(94)90167-8. Still the clearest statement that "convergence" must be defined explicitly (same site from independent origin vs. merely similar).

**Gap / what is new for us.** All frequency estimates above are for *catalytic* sites of enzymes with EC/CATH labels (Gherardini, Riziotis) or for pocket shape space with no phylogeny at all (Gao & Skolnick). No study estimates, over a large unbiased pocket set, the fraction of pocket-pair similarity attributable to shared ancestry vs convergence *with an explicit homology control and a randomised background*. That is Part A, and the Gao/Skolnick background and the Riziotis analogue pairs give us both the null and the positives.

## 3. Pocket similarity methods and benchmarks

- **Shulman-Peleg, Nussinov & Wolfson 2004** (SiteEngine), J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2004.04.012 — sequence-order-independent, physicochemical site matching.
- **Kinoshita & Nakamura 2005**, Protein Sci, doi:10.1110/ps.041080105 — surface-patch matching against all PDB hetero-atom sites, with a similarity threshold calibrated on free/complexed pairs.
- **Konc & Janežič 2010** (ProBiS), Bioinformatics, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btq100; web server doi:10.1093/nar/gkq479. Local structural alignment of surfaces; used by Zillmer & Walther for cross-species pocket comparison.
- **Chartier & Najmanovich 2015** (IsoMIF), J Chem Inf Model, doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.5b00333 — molecular-interaction-field similarity detected across protein families; validated on six datasets. (Finder server: doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btv616.)
- **Simonovsky & Meyers 2020** (DeeplyTough), J Chem Inf Model, doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.9b00554. CNN pocket descriptors; honest about the generalisation drop when transferring between independently constructed benchmarks.
- **Benchmarks.**
  - **TOUGH-M1** — Govindaraj & Brylinski 2018, BMC Bioinformatics, doi:10.1186/s12859-018-2109-2. 7,524 protein–drug complexes with Fpocket-predicted pockets; **505,116 positive pairs** (globally dissimilar proteins, TM-score < 0.4, chemically similar ligands at Tanimoto ≥ 0.7) plus a negative subset. Also re-uses the **APoc** set (34,970 subject / 20,744 control pairs). Data: https://osf.io/6ngbs/ (free, OSF). **This is the convergence-detection benchmark for us: by construction its positives are pocket similarity without global structural similarity.**
  - **ProSPECCTs** — Ehrt, Brinkjost & Koch 2018, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006483. Ten tailor-made sets probing distinct failure modes (identical sequences different structures; decoys with 5-residue physicochemical changes, 13,430 vs 67,150 pairs; the **Kahraman** cofactor set, 920/5,480 pairs without phosphate sites, 1,320/8,680 complete; the **Barelier** set of unrelated proteins binding the same ligand). Download: http://www.ewit.ccb.tu-dortmund.de/ag-koch/prospeccts/ (per-file tarballs, click-through licence; `kahraman_structures.tar.gz` 6.7 MB, `review_structures.tar.gz` 77.5 MB, `barelier_structures.tar.gz` 22.8 MB).
  - **Kahraman set** — Kahraman, Morris, Laskowski & Thornton 2007, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2007.01.086: pockets binding the *same* ligand vary in shape more than ligand conformational variability explains, and pockets are on average **three times larger** than their ligand. Follow-up, Kahraman et al. 2010, Proteins, doi:10.1002/prot.22633: identical ligands sit in markedly different physicochemical environments in unrelated proteins. **Direct warning for pocket-similarity thresholds and for inferring ligand identity from pocket shape.**
  - **Vertex/SiteHopper set** — Chen et al. 2016, J Chem Inf Model, doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.6b00118. Positive/negative protein pairs defined by shared active ligands. Sequence alignment of pocket residues wins overall, but shape comparison wins for unrelated proteins with only 20–30% pocket-residue identity. **The quantitative statement of when homology-based matching stops working and pocket shape takes over — exactly our ortholog vs convergence boundary.**
  - **LIGYSIS** — Utgés & Barton 2024, J Cheminform, doi:10.1186/s13321-024-00923-z. ~30,000 proteins; aggregates biologically relevant unique protein–ligand interfaces across biological assemblies of multiple structures of the same protein, rather than 1:1 complexes. Benchmarks 13 predictors; best recall ~60% (fpocket+PRANK / DeepPocket), worst 39%; proposes top-N+2 recall as the standard metric. Data/code: doi:10.5281/zenodo.13121414, https://github.com/bartongroup/LBS-comparison. Companion: Utgés et al. 2024, Commun Biol, doi:10.1038/s42003-024-05970-8 — fragment-screening sites cluster into four classes; a cluster-C1 site is **28× more likely** to be functional than a C4 site.
  - **PLINDER** *(preprint)* — Durairaj et al. 2024, bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2024.07.17.603955. Largest curated protein–ligand interaction dataset with leakage-aware splits. Code Apache-2.0 (relicensed from GPL-2.0); current release `2026-09`, served from https://cameo3d.org/plinder/PLINDER-2026-09/ via the `plinder` PyPI package.
  - **PoseBusters** — Buttenschoen, Morris & Deane 2024, Chem Sci, doi:10.1039/d3sc04185a. Physical-validity checks for predicted poses; the standard sanity filter for anything we co-fold.
  - **Binding MOAD** — Hu, Benson, Smith & Carlson 2005, Proteins, doi:10.1002/prot.20512 (update: Smith et al. 2019, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2019.05.024). **PDBbind** — Wang et al. 2004, J Med Chem, doi:10.1021/jm030580l (now distributed as PDBbind+ under separate academic/commercial terms; verify licence before use).

**Gap / what is new for us.** Every benchmark above scores pocket-pair similarity in a phylogeny-free setting; none contains ortholog pairs with known divergence times, and none has negative controls for *pocket absence*. We will need to construct an ortholog-pair benchmark ourselves (see §Datasets), using TOUGH-M1/ProSPECCTs only to calibrate the similarity scale and the unrelated-fold background.

## 4. Orthology and phylogenetic methods

- **Altenhoff et al. 2016**, Nat Methods, doi:10.1038/nmeth.3830, and **Nevers et al. 2022**, Nucleic Acids Res, doi:10.1093/nar/gkac330 — the Quest for Orthologs Orthology Benchmark Service (https://orthology.benchmarkservice.org), the standard way to compare orthology calls. QfO reference proteomes: https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/reference_proteomes/QfO/, current `QfO_release_2026_02.tar.gz` (2.2 GB; 20 GB "additional").
- **Altenhoff et al. 2024** (OMA), Nucleic Acids Res, doi:10.1093/nar/gkad1020. Hierarchical Orthologous Groups (HOGs), ancestral genome pages, plus the ecosystem: OMAmer (proteome mapping), **OMArk** (Nevers et al. 2025, Nat Biotechnol, doi:10.1038/s41587-024-02147-w) for proteome-quality assessment — OMArk detects not only incompleteness but **gene overprediction and contamination**, which is exactly the artifact class behind spurious "pocket gain".
- **Hernández-Plaza et al. 2023** (eggNOG 6.0), Nucleic Acids Res, doi:10.1093/nar/gkac1022. 17M OGs at 1,601 taxonomic levels over 12,535 organisms, with trees per OG and built-in phylogenetic-profile tooling.
- **Thomas et al. 2022** (PANTHER), Protein Sci, doi:10.1002/pro.4218 — curated gene family trees with ancestral nodes; **Emms & Kelly 2019** (OrthoFinder), Genome Biol, doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1832-y — de novo orthogroups + gene trees + rooted species tree when we need custom taxon sets.
- Gene/species-tree reconciliation: **Morel et al. 2020** (GeneRax), Mol Biol Evol, doi:10.1093/molbev/msaa141 — species-tree-aware ML gene trees under duplication/transfer/loss; **Szöllősi et al. 2013**, Syst Biol, doi:10.1093/sysbio/syt003 — transfers from unsampled/extinct lineages (28% of transfers in their cyanobacterial set show such signatures), a caution against reading every incongruence as biology.
- Ancestral state reconstruction for a binary trait (pocket present/absent):
  - **Lewis 2001**, Syst Biol, doi:10.1080/106351501753462876 — the Mk model, with the ascertainment correction for variable-only characters. Our default likelihood model for pocket presence.
  - **Csűrös 2010** (Count), Bioinformatics, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btq315 — Dollo and Wagner parsimony plus phylogenetic birth-and-death likelihood on phyletic profiles; BSD-style licence, Java.
  - **Cohen & Pupko 2010** (GLOOME), Bioinformatics, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btq549, and the simulation study Cohen & Pupko 2011, Genome Biol Evol, doi:10.1093/gbe/evr101 — stochastic mapping vs maximum parsimony for gain/loss; shows when parsimony misassigns events.
  - **Alekseyenko, Lee & Suchard 2008**, Syst Biol, doi:10.1080/10635150802434394 — stochastic Dollo, for traits that are hard to re-gain.
- Phylogenetic signal: **Pagel 1999**, Nature, doi:10.1038/44766 (λ); **Blomberg, Garland & Ives 2003**, Evolution, doi:10.1111/j.0014-3820.2003.tb00285.x (K, continuous traits); **Fritz & Purvis 2010**, Conserv Biol, doi:10.1111/j.1523-1739.2010.01455.x (**D**, the phylogenetic-signal statistic for *binary* traits, robust above ~50 taxa). **D is the right statistic for "do pockets descend by orthology?" — it is comparable across datasets and bounded by Brownian and random expectations.**
- Phylogenetic profiling: **Pellegrini et al. 1999**, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.96.8.4285 — co-occurrence patterns across genomes as functional evidence; the same formalism applies to pocket presence profiles.
- Ortholog conjecture: **Nehrt et al. 2011**, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002073 (challenged it) vs **Altenhoff et al. 2012**, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002514 (orthologs weakly but significantly more functionally similar than paralogs). The effect we expect for pockets is real but modest — so power matters, and ortholog/paralog contrasts should be pre-registered rather than selected post hoc.

**Gap / what is new for us.** Gain/loss machinery (Count, GLOOME, Mk/stochastic-Dollo) and signal statistics (D) exist and are mature, but they have only ever been applied to gene-family or morphological characters. Applying them to a *structurally derived, noisily observed* character — pocket presence from a predictor on a predicted structure — demands an explicit error model: the observation is not the trait. We must feed per-pocket confidence (pLDDT/PAE, pocket-finder score stability) into the character states or into a sensitivity analysis.

## 5. Co-folding: capabilities and limits

- **Abramson et al. 2024** (AlphaFold 3), Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w. Joint prediction of proteins with ligands, ions, nucleic acids; reported large gains over docking. Code Apache-2.0 (github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold3), **weights under a separate non-commercial terms-of-use** — relevant since we hold local weights.
- **Krishna et al. 2024** (RoseTTAFold All-Atom), Science, doi:10.1126/science.adl2528.
- **Wohlwend et al. 2024** (Boltz-1) *(preprint)*, bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2024.11.19.624167, MIT; **Passaro et al. 2025** (Boltz-2, adds an affinity module) *(preprint)*, bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2025.06.14.659707; **Chai-1** *(preprint)*, bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2024.10.10.615955, Apache-2.0; **Protenix** *(preprint)*, bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2025.01.08.631967, Apache-2.0.
- **Masters, Mahmoud & Lill 2025**, Nat Commun, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-63947-5 (preprint 2024, doi:10.1101/2024.06.03.597219). Adversarial tests of whether co-folding learns physics. From the full text: for ATP–CDK2, AF3 reproduces the native mode at 0.2 Å; in the **binding-site removal** challenge, where *all* site residues are replaced by glycine, **all four co-folding models keep predicting the same binding mode** despite losing every side-chain interaction; in the **packing** challenge (all site residues → phenylalanine) predictions remain heavily biased to the original site. The authors conclude poses come from global sequence/structure patterns, not interaction physics.
- **Škrinjar, Eberhardt, Durairaj & Schwede 2025** *(preprint)*, "Have protein–ligand cofolding methods moved beyond memorisation?", bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2025.02.03.636309, also Research Square doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-7311192/v1. Benchmark "Runs N' Poses": **2,600 high-resolution systems released after the training cutoffs** of four leading all-atom methods; conclusion: current methods largely memorise poses from training data.
- **Kim, Correy, … Shoichet & Fraser 2026**, eLife reviewed preprint doi:10.7554/eLife.110475.2 (published 2026-09-24). Prospective evaluation on **551 post-cutoff SARS-CoV-2 Mac1 ligand complexes (489 evaluable)**: AF3, Boltz-2 and Chai-1 each reproduce >50% of poses to <2 Å, **but** none recapitulates the conformational rearrangements (peptide flip, loop opening) that accompany binding; AF3 ligand-pose confidence **does not separate true ligands from high-scoring false positives** as well as docking scores or Boltz-2 affinity predictions do, across AmpC β-lactamase, D4 and σ2 prospective sets.
- **Karelina, Noh & Dror 2023**, eLife, doi:10.7554/eLife.89386. AF2 models capture GPCR pocket *structures* nearly as well as cross-ligand experimental structures, yet docking into AF2 models is no better than into homology models and much worse than into experimental apo structures. **Accurate pocket geometry does not imply usable pose prediction.**

**Gap / what is new for us.** The literature converges on one point with direct design consequences: co-folding will place a ligand in a plausible-looking pocket even when the pocket has been destroyed, so **a confident co-folded complex is not evidence that a pocket exists, and a co-folding failure is weak evidence that it is lost**. Co-folding is admissible only as *relative* evidence under matched controls: ancestral vs derived sequence folded identically, a holo PDB positive control, decoy/non-cognate ligand negative controls, PoseBusters filtering, and the glycine/phenylalanine site-ablation control of Masters et al. run on our own cases. Nobody has yet used co-folding under this control regime to adjudicate evolutionary pocket gain/loss — that is ours to define.

## 7. Artifact sources for "lost" and "gained" pockets in AF models

1. **Confidence.** Akdel et al. 2022 (doi:10.1038/s41594-022-00849-w): low-confidence AF2 pockets (mean pocket pLDDT ≤ 90) are larger and generally fail to overlap known sites, while >90 pockets match experimental performance. Use mean pocket-residue pLDDT as a hard filter and report both strata; use PAE for inter-domain pockets, whose geometry depends on relative domain placement.
2. **Disorder.** Tunyasuvunakool et al. 2021, Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03828-1: 58% of human residues confidently predicted, 36% very high — the rest is largely disorder or low-confidence, where apparent cavities are meaningless. Ruff & Pappu 2021, J Mol Biol, doi:10.1016/j.jmb.2021.167208, on reading AF output for IDRs.
3. **Fragments and length limits.** The human AFDB release attempted only sequences of 16–2,700 residues (Tunyasuvunakool et al. 2021, Methods), so long proteins appear as fragments; a pocket missing from a fragmented ortholog is an indexing artifact, not a loss. Always check coverage of the pocket region against full-length UniProt.
4. **Gene-model errors.** Meyer et al. 2020, BMC Bioinformatics, doi:10.1186/s12859-020-03855-1: across ten primate proteomes (176,478 proteins), **82,305 potential errors** — 44,001 deletions, 27,289 insertions, 11,015 mismatched segments — affecting **up to 50%** of sequences. In non-model species this is the dominant cause of a truncated or garbled "lost pocket". Screen proteomes with **OMArk** (doi:10.1038/s41587-024-02147-w) and flag species with high fragmentation/overprediction.
5. **Species-specific structural clusters.** Barrio-Hernandez et al. 2023 (doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06510-w): ~4% of AFDB clusters are species-specific and are explicitly interpreted as either low-quality predictions or de novo gene birth. Lineage-restricted "gains" must be checked against this.
6. **Apo-like conformational bias.** Saldaño et al. 2022, Bioinformatics, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btac202: AF2 predicts the *holo* form in ~70% of apo/holo pairs, accuracy degrades with the conformational diversity of the family, and main-chain flexibility anticorrelates with pLDDT. So AF models are not uniformly apo — they are family-dependent, and the same family can be modelled in different states across orthologs, creating fake pocket turnover. The AHoJ-AlphaFold preprint (§1) quantifies this apo–holo continuum per pocket.
7. **Cryptic pockets.** A pocket absent from a single AF conformer may be present in the dynamic ensemble (Cimermancic 2016; Vajda 2018; Meller 2023). Run PocketMiner-style cryptic-site prediction on apparent losses before claiming loss.
8. **Pocket-finder instability.** Utgés & Barton 2024 (doi:10.1186/s13321-024-00923-z): top-ranked recall varies from 39% to 60% across current predictors on the same data, and redundant predictions degrade performance. Any single-tool presence/absence call is noisy; require agreement across settings and, ideally, across tools.

**Gap / what is new for us.** These seven artifact classes have each been documented separately; none has been assembled into a required negative-control cascade for evolutionary inference. Building that cascade — and reporting how many candidate gains/losses each filter removes — is itself a publishable result and the main defence of our case list.

**How to read these for Part A.** Riziotis gives the only direct homology-vs-analogy split for *catalytic sites with identical function* (≈10% homologous, ≈90% analogous) — but that ratio reflects M-CSA's curation, which deliberately spans folds, and must not be quoted as a proteome-wide convergence rate. Gherardini's 15% of EC groups is the better-grounded frequency statement. Gao & Skolnick supply the opposing bound: pocket similarity alone carries little evidence of common ancestry, because the pocket shape space is small and reachable without selection. Our Part A number must therefore be reported *relative to an explicit background* (randomised pocket pairs, fold-matched and fold-mismatched), not as a raw fraction of similar pairs.

### The three validation axes, concretely
1. **Pocket matching across orthologs.** No public ortholog-pocket benchmark exists. Build one: take LIGYSIS/BioLiP2 proteins that have experimental ligand sites in **two or more species of the same HOG**, project sites onto AFDB models via SIFTS, and require our matcher to recover the cross-species correspondence. Positive control: same protein, two species. Negative control: shuffled ortholog assignment within the same fold.
2. **Convergence detection.** TOUGH-M1 positives (globally dissimilar, similar ligand) and the Riziotis 34 curated analogue cases as positives; ProSPECCTs decoy sets and fold-matched random pairs as negatives; report the score distribution against the Gao–Skolnick background expectation rather than a bare threshold.
3. **Orthology quality.** OMA HOGs as primary, eggNOG 6.0 as the independent second opinion (disagreement flags a case), OMArk for per-proteome quality, and QfO benchmarking only if there is spare time.

### Things to avoid under time pressure
- PDBbind: licensing moved to PDBbind+ with separate academic/commercial terms; LIGYSIS and BioLiP2 cover our needs without that friction.
- Re-deriving orthologs with OrthoFinder from scratch: use precomputed HOGs; OrthoFinder only if a needed clade is missing.
- Treating any single-conformer pocket call, or any co-folding confidence score, as evidence on its own — see §5 and §7.
